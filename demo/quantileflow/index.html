Option position comparator on a fixed budget

Given a fixed budget, which whole contract is worth buying — and what happens if the market does not do what you hoped? This demo answers that with the same engine as QuantileFlow, ported to the browser.

Synthetic data. The option chain is generated here with Black–Scholes on an invented smile. There is no market data, no broker connection, nothing asks you for credentials, and no probabilities are estimated. None of this is a recommendation to buy.

2. Scenarios

Each scenario is an exit: how many days passed, how far the underlying moved and how much IV changed. Every PnL comes from repricing the whole contract under those conditions, not from a local approximation with the greeks. No probability is attached to any of them.

    4. PnL map

    Every cell reprices the whole position at the chosen number of days, moving the underlying (columns) and shifting IV (rows). Colour runs from loss to gain; the number is the net PnL in USD, with both commissions and the exit semispread already taken out.

    5. How it is built

    1. How many fit. One contract costs ask × multiplier + commission. The quantity is the integer part of budget ÷ (cost + commission): the closing commission is reserved too, so you are not left without cash on the way out. Series are never mixed and no optimal size is sought.
    2. What gets reserved. outlay = quantity × cost and reserved capital = outlay + quantity × commission. Whatever is left of the budget is free cash and earns no interest.
    3. What it earns. In each scenario the contract is repriced with Black–Scholes at IV + change and (days to expiry − days elapsed). You are assumed to exit one semispread below the theoretical price, except at expiry, where the payoff rules. Then PnL = quantity × (exit × multiplier − commission) − outlay.
    4. What wins the comparison. The main criterion is net PnL in the target scenario on the same budget. Separately the candidates are ranked by return on reserved capital and by robustness, which is the worst PnL on the list. If no candidate has a positive worst case, staying in cash wins.
    5. What it does not do. It attaches no probabilities to the scenarios, so the worst case on the list is not the maximum loss: a long option can lose the entire outlay. It places no orders and it is not a forecast.

    6. Cross-check against the Python engine

    The Python engine reprices with a PDE solver; this port uses the closed form of Black–Scholes. For European options with flat IV — the scope the engine itself declares — they are equivalent. These checks run in your browser against the reference cases from the repository.
